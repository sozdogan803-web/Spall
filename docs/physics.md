# Spall physics notes

What the program computes, with the equations and constants it uses. SI units throughout unless
stated. Source files are named for each part. Everything here is an educational approximation:
the constants are chosen to give plausible results across the whole catalogue, not to reproduce
any one test.

**Scope.** Outputs are penetration figures for generic plates. There are no damage, lethality,
casualty, vulnerability or targeting outputs. (An explosion viewer with blast-wave and fireball
physics was part of earlier versions; it was removed in October 2026 and its notes are in the git
history.)

## 1. Catalogue data (`data/`, `tools/`)

- `data/explosives.json` (36 fillers, used for the filler of shells and missiles) is generated by
  `tools/compute_explosives.py` from each filler's composition, density, detonation velocity D and relative effectiveness (RE).
  - **Products** by the Kistiakowsky–Wilson rules: Cl → HCl, H → H₂O, C → CO, then CO → CO₂ with
    the oxygen left; carbon left over is solid soot.
  - **Afterburn energy** per kg of filler: CO 283.0, C(s) 393.5 and H₂ 241.8 kJ/mol burned in air.
  - Check: TNT gives about 10.4 MJ/kg of afterburn energy and 0.185 kg/kg of soot.

## 2. Armour vs projectile (`src/physics/penetration.*`, `src/sim/armour_run.*`)

One projectile against a stack of up to 8 parallel plates (material, thickness, gap), at an
obliquity θ ≤ 80°. Line-of-sight thickness is t/cos θ. Materials have a density, a Tate target
resistance R_t, a Brinell hardness (for validity warnings) and a compact-AP efficiency; ceramics
are treated as laterally confined.

- **Long rods (APFSDS)**: Tate–Alekseevskii, integrated in time:
  ½ ρ_p (v − u)² + Y_p = ½ ρ_t u² + R_t, with rod deceleration dv/dt = −Y_p/(ρ_p L) and erosion
  dL/dt = −(v − u). Y_p = 1.5 GPa. The penetration rate is scaled by the Lanz–Odermatt (2000)
  length factor and cos θ^(−0.224), and R_t for steels is fitted to hardness so that the result
  matches Lanz–Odermatt for RHA. When the rod is too slow to erode it decelerates as a rigid body.
- **Compact AP shot**: de Marre limit velocity (K = 2300 m/s for RHA, thickness and diameter in
  dm, mass in kg), using the RHA-equivalent thickness from each material's efficiency; touching
  plates act as one block. Residual velocity by Lambert–Jonas: v_r = √(v² − v_L²); plugs are
  drawn but not tracked.
- **Shaped charges**: 600 jet elements with velocities from the tip velocity
  (7000 + 2000 q m/s, q = jet quality) down to 2000 m/s, all leaving a virtual origin at
  (standoff + 1) cone diameters. Each element stretches until the breakup time,
  (0.8 + 0.8 q) µs per mm of cone diameter; after breakup the particles drift off axis and only
  the fraction that stays inside the crater counts. Each element penetrates hydrodynamically (Tate with Y = 0):
  ρ_j (v − u)² = ρ_t u² + 2 R_t. The jet quality of a catalogue round is fitted to its published
  RHA penetration.
- **Derived figures**: outcome per layer, residual velocity, length and mass, ballistic limit
  (kinetic rounds), depth into a semi-infinite RHA witness block, areal density, RHA-equivalent
  thickness from depth of penetration, thickness and mass efficiency.
- Model warnings are shown when a case leaves a model's calibration range (e.g. rod L/D outside
  10–40, non-steel or very hard/soft plates, ceramics, spaced plates).

### Deformation simulation (`src/physics/mpm_material.*`, `src/sim/mpm_scene.*`, `src/sim/mpm_solver.*`, `shaders/mpm/`)

The Cross-section's **Deformation (MPM)** display simulates the projectile and the plates as
deformable material on the GPU. It is a visual companion to the engineering model, whose figures
stay authoritative.

- **Geometry.** The plane of the cross-section in plane strain: a slab of unit depth, so the
  rod is modelled as a blade. Simulated depths are therefore indicative and are shown beside the
  model's. x runs along the shot line from the first plate face, y is lateral. Plates are
  rotated by the obliquity; their half-extent along the face is max(8 D, 0.6 × line of sight),
  at most 0.5 m, and particles within two cells of each end are anchored. Next to each
  anchored strip is a mount band, max(6 cells, min(plate thickness, 0.2 × half-extent)) wide,
  standing for the mounting and the plate beyond the modelled width: it yields and stretches
  but never fails (no strain, spall, melt, fracture or delamination failure), so a hard hit
  bulges the plate between its mounts instead of tearing it off them and throwing it whole.
  A plane-strain hit loads the whole depth of the plate at once, so heavy rounds still
  push it far; the plate beyond the band can still tear, as an ERA flyer plate should.
- **Soft mounts** (off by default; `--mpm-soft-mounts` or the toolbar box). The continuum is
  already compliant; only the clamped edge is rigid. With soft mounts the anchored strip is not
  held in place but tethered: a damped spring body force pulls each strip particle towards its
  rest position, with a stiffness of a 1 m bar of steel (wave speed 5000 m/s) and a damping
  ratio of 0.5, so the mounting gives and rings instead of reflecting every wave at a wall. A
  strip held at the back face stays clamped, and tethered particles are not counted in the
  reactive, ceramic or composite readouts.
- **Blocks (ERA).** A layer with `block_count` n > 0 is n separate free blocks of the layer's
  width and gap, centred on its offset; 0 repeats tiles across the whole plate. A block is held
  by nothing, so the one the round hits is thrown off on its own. Dragging clamps a block group's
  reach to 0.2 m past the shot line plus half its span, so it cannot be lost beyond the window.
- **Burst cone.** A fuzed filler's products already push the case outwards; the extra, Taylor's
  tilt, leans the case beside the charge towards the way the detonation sweeps (forward for a
  base-lit shell, back for a nose-lit rocket) by V sin θ = V² / (2D), with V Gurney's cylinder
  speed √(2E) / √(M/C + ½) from the case and charge mass alongside each other. It is applied once
  per case particle as the front reaches it and is not momentum-balanced. For a thick-cased shell
  the tilt is modest (tens of m/s); the visible forward bias mostly comes from the round's own
  speed after it perforates.
- **Projectile.** A long rod of L ≥ 5 D is seeded as a generic fin-stabilised dart: the
  penetrator body, a hollow light-alloy ballistic cap 2.5 D long ahead of it, swept
  light-alloy fins (two in the plane; span 3.6 D tip to tip, root chord 3 D, tip chord 1.2 D)
  and a tracer pellet behind the tail. The fittings are simulated like the body but are left
  out of the rod readouts (nose, intact length, velocity, failure counts), and t = 0 stays the
  body's first contact. Shorter rods and compact shot keep their plain shapes.
- **Missiles.** A missile is seeded whole, part by part, from its catalogue shape, so it
  crumples as it explodes and impacts. A thin nose shell (wall max(0.03 D, 2 mm)) is hollow
  round the fuze; the warhead case holds the explosive filler; behind it the motor is a thin
  light-alloy tube (wall max(0.04 R, 0.8 mm)) closed by a nozzle plate with a throat, round a
  propellant grain; the fins are simulated, not only drawn. The grain is bored out to match the
  catalogue mass: a brittle solid (ρ 1700, crack strength 45 MPa, Weibull modulus 6) that
  breaks into pieces instead of carrying load. A warning is raised only if the parts alone
  exceed the mass, or if a full grain still cannot reach it. The nose-contact fuze lights on the
  mass-weighted momentum of all the missile's particles, and the Gurney burst uses the case-wall
  mass alongside the filler (fittings excluded). Within the short simulated window the motor
  tube behind the warhead stays largely intact; it would take milliseconds to stop.
- **Brittle fracture.** Hard parts (tungsten carbide, cap, case and core steels, the propellant)
  carry a crack strength and a Weibull modulus. Each particle draws a flaw factor from a Weibull
  distribution with mean one (a hash of its initial position, floored at 0.25, so runs repeat);
  the part cracks where the largest principal tension, 2G × (largest deviatoric log strain) plus
  the mean stress, exceeds the strength times the factor. A cracked particle takes the existing
  spall flag, so it becomes debris and shows as spall in the Failure view. The strengths are 1.2
  GPa (carbide, modulus 6), 2.0 GPa (cap, 12), 2.2 GPa (case, 14), 2.6 GPa (core, 16). They are
  approximate. Plates and tough rod metals are not brittle, so a 30 mm APFSDS into 600 mm of
  RHA mushrooms and stops at about 90 mm; making a catalogue plate material brittle from its
  JSON `mpm` block is not supported yet.
- **Method.** MLS-MPM with APIC transfer and quadratic B-splines (Hu et al. 2018), 2 × 2
  particles per cell. The explicit step is dt = 0.25 dx / (c_max + v), c = √((K + 4G/3)/ρ).
  The grid is a fixed-point int32 buffer filled with `atomicAdd`, so runs are deterministic.
  Advection uses a compensated (Fast2Sum) position so slow particles still move in float32.
- **Constitutive model.** Each particle carries the elastic deformation gradient Fe (2 × 2)
  and the out-of-plane stretch Fe_zz. Hencky (log-strain) elasticity with shear modulus G and
  bulk modulus K, and J2 plasticity by radial return in the three principal log strains. The
  yield stress is Johnson–Cook without the rate term: σy = (A + B εp^n)(1 − T*^m), with
  T* = (T − 293 K)/(T_melt − 293 K). Linear and quadratic bulk viscosity (0.2, 1.0) act in
  compression. `src/physics/mpm_material.cpp` is the CPU reference of the return map and is
  unit tested.
- **Heating and failure.** ΔT = 0.9 σy Δεp / (ρ c_p). A particle fails when εp > ε_f (plastic
  strain), when its tensile mean stress exceeds the spall strength (spall), or when T ≥ T_melt
  (melt). A failed particle keeps its mass and momentum but carries no shear and no tension, so
  it moves as debris.
- **Materials.** Each armour material has an `mpm` block in `data/armour_materials.json` (G, K,
  A, B, n, m, T_melt, c_p, ε_f, spall), or falls back to its group's defaults. Penetrators use
  built-in tungsten-alloy, DU and hard-steel sets chosen by the rod material. A block may name
  its constitutive model, `"model": "metal"` (the model above), `"ceramic"`, `"elastomer"` or
  `"composite"`; without one it keeps its fallback's (ceramic for the ceramic group, metal
  otherwise), and keys its model does not use are rejected. The parameters are generic,
  approximate open-literature values (confidence low). On the GPU each material is 8 vec4
  (`mpmPackMaterial`).
- **Ceramics (JH-2 form, no rate term or bulking).** From the Hugoniot elastic limit and the
  moduli, uniaxial strain gives ε_H = HEL / (K + 4G/3), P_HEL = K ε_H, σ_HEL = 2G ε_H. The
  strength at pressure p is σ = σ_i − D (σ_i − σ_f), with the intact σ_i = σ_HEL A ((p + T) /
  P_HEL)^N and the fractured σ_f = min(σ_HEL B (p / P_HEL)^M, σ_fmax σ_HEL) (zero for p ≤ 0).
  The deviatoric strain is returned onto σ; plastic strain grows the fracture variable D by
  Δεp / ε_f(p), ε_f = max(D1 ((p + T) / P_HEL)^D2, 10^−4), and tension past T (1 − D) sets
  D = 1. At D = 1 the ceramic is comminuted: a granular solid with the fractured strength and no
  tension. Melting fails it. Alumina, silicon carbide, boron carbide and glass have approximate
  published fits; other ceramic-group materials fall back to an alumina-like set with
  HEL = 2 × the Tate resistance.
- **Elastomers.** Compressible neo-Hookean in the principal stretches, τ_i = G J^(−2/3)
  (λ_i² − Σλ²/3) + K ln J, with no plasticity, plus a deviatoric Kelvin–Voigt viscous stress
  2 η J D_dev whose von Mises value is capped at a viscous limit; its work heats the rubber.
  It fails when its largest principal engineering stretch exceeds the failure strain, by spall,
  or at its decomposition temperature. The step also keeps dt ≤ 0.25 ρ dx² / η. Rubber, a
  generic polyurea/polyurethane entry and the gelatin test medium (below) use it. Once failed it
  keeps no deviatoric strain and carries compression only, so it flows like a fluid.
- **Fibre composites (aramid, UHMWPE).** A laminate with its plies along the plate face. Each
  particle carries a fibre vector f, seeded as the unit face tangent and carried with the
  material, f ← (I + dt C) f, whose length is the fibre stretch λ_f. The fibres add a
  tension-only Kirchhoff stress E_f ln λ_f f̂ ⊗ f̂ and break for good above the fibre failure
  strain. The matrix takes the metal model with a low yield. It delaminates when the Cauchy
  stress across the plies exceeds the through-thickness strength, the shear on them exceeds
  the interlaminar strength, or the matrix reaches its failure strain or spall stress.
  Delaminated, it is a stack of sheets: its elastic strain is taken in the ply frame without
  the ply shear (the sheets slide on each other), each sheet still yields like the matrix, and
  any tension across the plies is relaxed away (they open), while the fibres still carry
  tension. Delaminated with broken fibres, or melted, it has failed. The step uses
  √((K + 4G/3 + E_f) / ρ).
- **Reactive layers (generic ERA sandwich).** A material marked `"reactive": true` (the
  catalogue's generic sheet explosive) also has a detonation speed D, an energy Q, a products
  γ and an initiation pressure in its `mpm` block. An unreacted particle lights when its
  pressure −K ln J (without bulk viscosity, so impact ringing does not count) exceeds the
  initiation pressure. The first one to light starts a programmed front that runs through its
  layer from its initial position at D (an `atomicCompSwap` picks it; the others read the front
  from the next step on); a particle lights when the front reaches its initial position. Its
  reacted fraction grows as λ = D (t − t_light) / (2 dx) up to 1, and it becomes a fluid (no
  shear, no tension) with the pressure of gamma-law products released at constant volume,
  p = λ (γ − 1) ρ0 Q J^−γ + (1 − λ) K max(−ln J, 0); expanding from J = 1 does work ρ0 Q per
  unit volume, so Q acts as the Gurney energy. ln J is capped at 4.6. The products temperature
  is 293 K + λ (Q / c_p) J^(−1/4) (display only), the fireball law of the removed explosion viewer: the gamma-law
  isentrope J^(1−γ) would take them below red heat by J = 2, which real products, whose γ falls
  towards 1.3 as they expand, do not. The time step uses max(c, D, 1.5 √(γ (γ − 1) Q))
  for reactive materials. The engineering model treats the layer as an inert soft plate (a
  warning says so).
- **Domain and budget.** The domain spans from the rod's tail to past the stack (residual travel
  up to 1.5 rod lengths), with half a plate extent of room above and below for bulging and
  debris. It is at most 2048 cells per axis (3072 for Ultra, 4096 for Insane, 6144 for
  Ludicrous). Quality sets dx = D/8, D/14 or D/22 (Low, Medium, High), coarsened to a particle
  budget of 150 k, 600 k or 1.6 M at four particles per cell. Ultra, Insane and Ludicrous are for
  slow motion and pre-rendering (Insane and Ludicrous are best pre-rendered: a step takes about
  15 ms at Insane, so a shot is computed once and then watched at any speed): they ask for
  D/128, D/256 and D/512, which the grid never holds, so the grid and the budget (4 M, 12 M and
  100 M) set the cell. A case whose grid is mostly empty (a long rod) seeds more particles to a
  cell to spend the budget, up to 6 x 6, 9 x 9 and 16 x 16, which gives smoother material and
  finer debris but no more resolution than the cell has. A level whose scene would need more than 85 % of the
  free video memory (24 B a grid node, 144 B a particle) is not given up on. The particles are
  held in chunks of at most 512 MiB (and at most one storage block the driver allows, 2 GB on
  the tested NVIDIA card, so no scene is limited to that), the grid stays in video memory, and
  the chunks that do not fit video memory wait in system memory, up to 60 % of what is free.
  The "System RAM" box in the deformation controls turns this off, and then a level that
  does not fit gives way to the next one down, as does one that does not fit even with the
  system memory (the app says why). Spilling gives the GPU more room, not the CPU any of the
  arithmetic: every step the GPU copies a spilled chunk up through a window of video memory,
  steps it and copies it back (`glCopyBufferSubData`, measured at about 28 GB/s up and 13 GB/s
  there and back on the tested card), so a gigabyte spilled adds roughly 70 to 150 ms a step.
  Measured on the 16 GB RTX 5070 Ti: Ludicrous (83 million particles in a 5390 x 6141 grid,
  12.0 GB) loads in 7.6 s and runs entirely in video memory at 106 ms a step; with the card
  limited to 8 GB by the self-test (`SPALL_MPM_FREEVIDEO_GB=8`) it is cut into 23 chunks, 11
  in video memory and 12 (5.7 GB) in system memory, and a step takes 770 ms, which is about
  117 ms for each spilled gigabyte. Insane (11 million particles) takes 14.7 ms and Ultra
  (2.5 million) 3.7 ms a step, both entirely in video memory.
  Reading the host buffers from the shader directly was 76 times slower and was not used.
  Which particles sit in which chunk, and whether a chunk is in video or system memory, cannot
  change the result: the grid is fixed-point integer atomics, so a scene cut into five chunks
  with two of them spilled runs bit for bit as the one buffer does (self-test check 20, a
  reactive sandwich and an APHE round through the detonation and the fuze). The projectile's
  chunks are drawn last so it is never hidden by the target. A spilled run keeps no
  checkpoints (a state copy would have to cross the same bus), so it scrubs back by starting
  again; pre-render it. Where the driver does not report free video memory (only NVIDIA's
  query is used) everything stays in video memory and nothing is spilled. Particles that
  reach the domain edge, or go non-finite, are retired;
  the readouts count the latter.
- **Playback.** Until the nose is three cells from the first face the projectile flies rigidly
  and is drawn shifted by v·Δt. Each frame then steps the simulation up to the playback time
  within a 14 ms budget, and the clock waits while the simulation is behind. Seeking backwards
  restarts it. The simulation runs to 1.25 × the engineering model's event time.
- **Display of products.** In the Material view each products particle is splatted as a
  ragged noise puff (growing as √J, at most 8 ×, never under 10 px, mass-conserving) into a
  float buffer of density, density × temperature and density × expansion. A full-screen pass
  lays it over the background, half of it blurred from its mipmaps so particles the simulation
  has left apart read as a thin haze: smoke of opacity 1 − exp(−τ) with τ proportional to the
  density, sooty when dense and greyer when expanded, shaded by the smoke above it; and light
  from the mean temperature through the display glow table (`src/render/blackbody.h`, a
  blackbody chromaticity with a brightness that is 0 below about 780 K), weighted by the
  opacity squared so the dense core reads as flame and the thin edges as smoke. The solid
  material is drawn over it; then hot metal (by the same table) adds a soft halo. The tracer pellet is drawn as plain
  material and has no glow of its own.
  The field views draw products as soft round sprites, and the Failure view shows them as
  "reacted".
- **Readouts.** Nose position, intact length and velocity of the intact penetrator material
  along the shot line, the furthest penetrator material, the peak temperature, von Mises stress
  and plastic strain, the failed particle counts, the fastest intact plate material and, with a
  reactive layer, the fraction of the explosive reacted.
- **Checks** (`--selftest mpm`): free flight keeps its momentum and moves v·t; with zero
  strength a rod's crater depth is near the hydrodynamic limit L √(ρp/ρt) (accepted between 1.1
  and 1.9 for 1.50; currently 1.32); a 10 mm plate is perforated and a 240 mm block is not. For
  the generic 4/4/4 mm sandwich: untouched, nothing reacts or moves; lit at its centre, its
  plates reach 0.6–1.3 × the symmetric-sandwich Gurney speed √(2Q) (M/C + 1/3)^−½, M the mass of
  both plates (currently 764 against 861 m/s; about 76 % of the explosive's energy ends up in the
  plates' motion after 20 µs); hit by the rod at 60°, at least half of it reacts and the plates
  away from the impact move faster than 300 m/s (currently all of it, 849 m/s). The rod into a
  ceramic sandwich (10 mm high-hardness steel, 60 mm alumina, 30 mm RHA) starts comminution at
  the shot line: with the nose 0.8 of the way through the cover, the ceramic within 1.5 D of the
  shot line is more than half comminuted and the ceramic far from it (0.5–0.9 of the plate
  extent) less than half as much (currently 77 % / 26 %); by the end all of the ceramic near the
  shot line is comminuted (100 % / 99 %: JH-2 damage is local, so crack bands spread across the
  narrow tile, and the check does not ask the far ceramic to survive). Into a bulging sandwich
  (6 mm RHA, 6 mm rubber, 6 mm RHA at 60°) the rubber stays finite and the plates bulge apart
  around the hole (12.0 mm at 1–2 D, 7.9 mm at 2–4 D, 3.1 mm far from it). Into ceramic on a
  composite backing (20 mm alumina, 25 mm UHMWPE) the laminate starts to delaminate around the
  shot line (74 % / 17 % with the nose halfway through the tile; the dart's cap strikes first),
  and by the end its fibres are broken near the shot line but not far from it (44 % / 0 %),
  while its weak through-thickness strength lets the plane-strain waves delaminate all of it.
  No particle goes non-finite in any run.
- **Display of the new materials.** Ceramic darkens as it fractures; comminuted ceramic and
  delaminated laminate are drawn smaller, like debris. The Failure view adds "comminuted"
  (blended in by D), "delaminated" and "fibre break", and the readouts give the fractions of
  ceramic comminuted and of laminate delaminated and with broken fibres. These are material
  states only.
- **Shaped-charge jets.** A shaped charge runs as a generic copper stream defined only by the
  engineering model's own parameters (cone diameter, standoff, jet quality, liner density); the
  liner's collapse is not simulated. At the model's animation start t_s = 0.25 t_0 the jet is
  seeded as rows across a plane-strain sheet from v_min t_s − S to v_tip t_s − S (S the virtual
  origin), each row moving at the model's velocity (x + S) / t_s, with the matching lateral
  inflow, at an approximate 700 K, from a built-in OFHC-like copper whose spall stress is out of
  reach (the seeded sheet's sides collapse and rebound at up to a few km/s, which would
  otherwise tear it apart). Its breakup is programmed from the model: the jet is cut into
  segments about 4 jet radii long at t_b, and at t_b every segment's first row (its neck) fails
  and every particle takes its segment's lateral kick (drift × the segment's velocity × a fixed
  pseudo-random offset), so the segments part as their velocity spread pulls them apart and
  drift sideways as single pieces. Jets are drawn copper; the readouts give the jet tip against
  the model's crater bottom, the fastest jet material against the model's element at the bottom,
  the fraction of the jet still intact, and the breakup time.
- **Jet checks** (`--selftest mpm`, generic 80 mm copper cone, q = 0.5): at 14 cone diameters
  of standoff the free jet is one piece at 0.9 t_b, 521 mm long against the model's stretching
  of 518 mm, and at 1.5 t_b, before reaching the plate, all of it is broken with every neck
  failed, its segments drift at 0.53 of their kicks (the copper's strength takes about half),
  its x momentum is kept to 0.04 % and its fastest material moves at 7762 m/s against the
  8000 m/s tip. At 2 cone diameters into a 600 mm RHA block it reaches 234 mm against the
  model's 375 mm (accepted between 0.4 and 1.1 of the model's; 155 mm at Low, as the sheet is
  only a few cells wide), and through a 20 mm plate its material leaves the back at 7009 m/s
  (model residual tip 7395 m/s). No particle goes non-finite or is retired.
- **Gelatin blocks and witness sheets.** Two more generic armour materials, both ordinary
  entries in `data/armour_materials.json` with no code of their own, give the cross-section a
  soft test medium and a thin indicator plate behind the armour. They are material states
  only: there is no body model and no output beyond the usual particle readouts.
  - *Ballistic gelatin* (`ballistic_gelatin`, 10 % gelatin at 4 °C, confidence low) is an
    elastomer with ρ = 1040 kg/m³, a water-like bulk modulus K = 2.4 GPa and a shear modulus
    of G = 30 kPa, fitted to open-literature quasi-static and low-rate data (Kwon & Subhash
    2010; Salisbury & Cronin 2009). A capped viscosity (η = 20 Pa·s, limit 2 MPa) stands in
    for its strong rate stiffening, which the model has no term for. It tears when the largest
    principal stretch exceeds 1.8 (failure strain 0.8), after which it flows; the spall stress
    is a conservative 5 MPa so the compression waves of a fast hit do not tear it by
    themselves; the "melt" temperature (450 K) is only a thermal breakdown limit, not gelatin
    melting. Because G is thousands of times below any other shipped material, the
    `shear_gpa` lower bound of the projectile and material loader is 1 kPa (10⁻⁶ GPa) rather
    than 100 kPa; 10⁻⁸ and negative values are still rejected. The viscous step limit keeps
    the scene's dt at or below `mpmViscousStep`.
  - *Aluminium 2024-T3* (`al2024`, a witness sheet) is a Johnson–Cook metal (G = 27.5 GPa,
    K = 71.6 GPa, A = 369 MPa, B = 684 MPa, n = 0.73, m = 1.7, T_melt = 775 K, ε_f = 0.6,
    spall 1.1 GPa; Lesuer 2000; Rosenberg & Dekel 2016).
  - *Presets*: "Ballistic gelatin block" (one free block, 200 mm wide and 250 mm deep),
    "Witness sheet" (12 mm RHA, a 150 mm gap and 1.5 mm of 2024), and "witness sheet and
    gelatin block" (the same, then a 100 mm gap and the block). The block layer is a free,
    unanchored block like the reactive tiles: drag it along or off the shot line (clamped to
    0.2 m off the axis). The thin sheet is a full-width layer clamped at its edges (or soft
    mounted) that can be dragged along the depth axis; the existing tests of the shipped
    materials and presets cover both.
  - *Checks*: the materials and presets load and validate (elastomer with the stated moduli,
    block unanchored, sheet clamped and thin, gelatin dt within the viscous limit), and 3 × 10⁻⁵
    GPa loads with a Pa-scale shear. `--selftest mpm` runs the 10 mm rod through 12 mm RHA, a
    1.5 mm 2024 sheet 150 mm behind it and a free 200 mm gelatin block 100 mm further back
    (394 000 particles, no particle non-finite, none of the block anchored): the sheet is torn
    over 100 % of the area within 2 D of the shot line and 7 % far from it (its clamped edge
    holds); with the nose 40 mm into the gelatin the first 30 mm of it are 75 % failed within
    2 D of the shot line and 0 % at 6–9.5 cm. By the end (351 µs) all of the block has failed:
    per metre of depth the plane-strain blade carries about 10⁴ times the energy gelatin needs
    to tear (a few tens of J/kg), so the whole slab is shoved aside, which the check does not
    ask it to avoid. The 30 mm APFSDS rod passes through a gelatin block (exit about 1.29 km/s
    in the Medium view) leaving a teardrop cavity and a spray of failed gel; a 7.62 mm
    carbide-core round into the block is shattered by the brittle-core model in 2D plane
    strain, whereas the engineering model has it perforate; the view does not tune that away.
- **Screens, a glacis, a perforated plate and stacked witness sheets.** Five more presets in
  `data/armour_materials.json` need no code, because free block layers (`block_width_mm`,
  `block_gap_mm`, `block_count`) already cut a layer into separate pieces and a steep obliquity
  already tilts a plate:
  - "Slat screen" (seven 8 mm x 30 mm RHA slats, 30 mm gaps, 300 mm in front of a 40 mm RHA
    hull) and "Bar screen" (five 20 mm square bars, 50 mm gaps, 400 mm in front of the hull).
    The odd count puts the middle piece on the shot line; drag the pieces along the face to
    shoot a gap or the edge of a piece instead (clamped to 0.2 m from the axis). Pieces are
    free, so a hit one is knocked aside and the others stay.
  - "Sloped glacis": 20 mm HHA over 60 mm RHA at 65 degrees, so the line of sight is about 190
    mm through 80 mm of plate.
  - "Perforated plate": nine 20 mm strips of 15 mm HHA with 10 mm slots between them, 150 mm in
    front of a 40 mm RHA plate. In a 2D cross-section a row of holes is a row of slots, so the
    plate is cut into strips of unit depth, not drilled with round holes.
  - "Witness stack": 12 mm RHA, then three 1.5 mm 2024 sheets 100 mm apart, each clamped at its
    edges like the single sheet.
  - *Checks*: every shipped preset builds a supported scene (a test loops over all of them), and
    each new preset is checked for its block counts, pitch, seeded gaps, free (unanchored)
    pieces, sheet thickness and spacing, and a particle count per layer.
- **Witness readouts, case files and CSV export** (`src/sim/witness_readout.*`,
  `src/sim/armour_case_io.*`, `src/app/armour_ui.cpp`). Small workflow aids in the armour view;
  none changes the simulation.
  - *Witness sheet*: a layer after the first that is at most 5 mm thick, sits at least 20 mm
    behind the layer in front of it and is not a block layer (`isWitnessSheet`). The
    "Read the sheets" button in the deformation readouts reads the particles back from the GPU
    (from the pre-rendered frame at the time shown, or from the live simulation) and reports,
    per sheet: the **hole width**, the **bulge** and the **residual velocity**. The hole is the
    run of failed sheet material that touches the shot line, sorted by where it started and
    followed while neighbours lie within two cells, plus one particle spacing; it is the extent
    across the shot line (along y), so under obliquity it is the projected width. The bulge is
    the largest forward displacement of sheet material that is still whole, and the fastest
    speed is the largest forward velocity of that material. The residual velocity is the
    penetrator's velocity once its nose is past the sheet and part of it is intact. These are
    material-state readings only.
  - *Reading back is on demand*, never per frame: it stalls the GPU and moves 144 bytes per
    particle, so the button is disabled while pre-rendering and above 3 million particles. A
    pre-rendered frame keeps fewer fields than the live state (positions, velocities, flags,
    material, plastic strain, temperature, burn and fracture, packed in half precision), which
    is all the readout uses; at the finest qualities it also keeps only one particle in
    `stride` (see the pre-render frames below), so the readout is of that sample. A note says when the simulation has moved on since the reading.
  - *Case files*: the Cases button saves the physical setup (projectile, custom penetrator,
    rod material, obliquity, velocity, layers) as JSON in `cases/<name>.json` next to the data
    folder, and loads it back. There is no file dialog, so the name is typed and the folder is
    fixed. The file stores the setup only, not the view, quality or soft-mount choices; saving
    over a name replaces it and says so. A load refuses a file over 1 MB and reports a parse
    error in the popup instead of changing the setup. Names are lower-cased, runs of anything
    but letters, digits and `-` become `_`, and they are at most 48 characters.
  - *CSV export*: Export CSV in the history panel writes the engineering model's time histories
    to `exports/<projectile>_<preset or edited>_<time stamp>.csv`, and the deformation
    simulation's readouts (time, nose, tail, reach, residual length, velocity, momentum,
    fastest speed, hottest, stress, plastic strain, momentum, counts of intact particles and of failures by cause,
    comminuted, delaminated and fibre-broken counts, jet and fuze readings) to `..._deformation.csv`. A pre-rendered run has a row for every
    frame; a live run is thinned to about 800 rows over the animation (`mpmLogMeasure`), kept
    in time order, and trimmed when the time is moved back.
- Pre-render frames. A pre-render steps the solver through 90 to 240 even times and packs the
  particles at each into a 40-byte frame (`shaders/mpm/frame.glsl`; `src/render/mpm_snapshots.*`),
  which the view draws with the positions interpolated between the two frames a playback time
  falls between. At the finest qualities a frame of every particle would be gigabytes (80 million
  particles is 3.2 GB), so a frame keeps one particle of every `stride` consecutive ones, picked
  by a hash of the particle's index (`shaders/mpm/pack.comp`) so that the kept ones do not line up
  with the scene's rows and columns, and the smallest `stride` (1 to 32) is used that gives at
  least 90 frames of at most 16 million particles. The frames get half the free video memory (one
  to four GiB); those that do not fit wait in system memory (half of what is free, at most
  16 GiB) and two cache slots of video memory hold the pair being played, copied up when the
  playback time moves on. Playing at a high quality therefore shows a sample of the particles,
  not every one; the readouts and the times are of the whole run. A frame does not depend on how
  the scene was chunked or where the frames are kept (self-test check 21).
- Scrubbing backwards. A pre-rendered run plays any frame at once. A live run keeps checkpoints
  (`MpmCheckpoints`, `src/sim/mpm_checkpoints.*`): full copies of the particle buffer, the
  detonation front and the fuze sums, with the step count, taken at up to 32 even times inside
  the run as the solver passes them (`MpmSolver::saveState` / `restoreState`, a GPU buffer copy
  each). A seek back resumes from the latest checkpoint at or before the time asked (give or
  take half a step) and steps on from it within the frame budget, so a drag to an earlier time
  costs at most one checkpoint interval of steps; with none that early (or none that fit) the run
  starts again from its first step. The steps use fixed-point integer atomics on the grid, so a
  replay from a checkpoint is the run it took the first time, bit for bit (self-test check 19
  compares every particle field for a reactive sandwich and an APHE round, through the
  detonation and the fuze). They take a quarter of the free video memory, between 256 MiB and
  2 GiB; a state that does not fit once means none are kept, and running out partway keeps those
  already taken. They are dropped when the case is rebuilt, while a pre-render runs (it needs the
  memory), when it completes (the frames take over), when the view leaves the deformation view,
  and re-planned when the animation's end moves. Reverse playback replays at most one interval
  per frame, so at the finest qualities it plays backwards in steps; pre-render for smooth
  reverse play.

## 3. Limits

- Gelatin is a calibrated soft medium, not a tissue or body simulant: it has no strain-rate
  hardening beyond the capped viscosity, no fracture toughness, no cavitation (the cavity is
  gel that failed and flowed), and no anisotropy or temperature dependence. In a 2D plane-strain
  slab its cavity is a long blade rather than a round temporary cavity, so cavity depth, width
  and the residual velocity of what passes through are indicative only. The witness sheet is a
  plain 2024 plate: it shows how far debris pushes and tears a thin plate, not a certified
  witness-pack test.
- Witness readouts: the hole width is the extent of the failed run across the shot line (in a
  2D slab that is a slot, not a round hole, and under obliquity a projection); it is zero when
  the sheet is not torn through at the shot line, and it counts only material that has failed,
  so a stretched but whole sheet reports a bulge and no hole. Reading back is capped at 3
  million particles, and from a pre-render at a stride above 1 it is of the kept sample only
  (a hole narrower than the spacing of the sample can be missed). CSV rows from a live run are thinned, so a fast spike between two rows
  can be missed; pre-render for every frame.

- Deformation view, jets: no liner collapse, slug or jet formation; the jet is a plane-strain
  copper sheet, not a round jet; its breakup time and drift are programmed from the engineering
  model, not predicted; its temperature is a guess; simulated depths are indicative and depend
  on the resolution.

- Penetration models are engineering fits for plates at normal-to-high obliquity; they do not cover
  reactive or composite armour beyond plate stacks.
- The deformation simulation is two-dimensional (plane strain): the penetrator is a blade, so
  plates flex and are cut further than a round rod would push them. It has no strain-rate
  hardening, and its material constants are generic.
- Ceramics have no strain-rate term, bulking or crack orientation: JH-2 damage is a local
  scalar, so in a narrow tile the comminuted region spreads as bands to the edges.
- A free laminate in plane strain has no clamping and no third dimension to spread its load:
  it delaminates throughout and drapes over the nose as a back-face cone. Delaminated sheets
  have no friction between them.
- Reactive layers use a programmed burn with gamma-law products: no reaction kinetics, no
  failure to initiate or partial detonation, no shock-to-detonation run distance. The products
  are resolved only by the particle spacing, so thin sheets are coarse, and the plates' anchored
  ends hold them back.
